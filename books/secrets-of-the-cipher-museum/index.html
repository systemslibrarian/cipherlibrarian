The stories behind the secrets

A secret becomes readable.
What happens next?

Unidentified letters in a French archive restore a queen’s voice. A British admiral must reveal a German proposal without exposing his service’s access. A codebreaker reads a smuggler’s orders after the chance to intervene has passed.

Across twenty investigations, Secrets of the Cipher Museum follows hidden messages into the decisions they make possible—and the questions they leave unresolved. The recovery of Copiale in 2011, the Zodiac 340 cipher in 2020 and Mary Stuart’s letters in 2023 brings the story into the work of modern researchers.

Who can be trusted with a secret? What does a deciphered message actually prove? When should someone act on what they know—and what might that action expose?

Eight original diagrams and four documentary images make crucial ideas visible. Chapter links and QR codes lead into the free Cipher Museum, where you can compare readings, test an assumption and inspect what a published recovery establishes. The historical narrative is complete without a browser.

An opening excerpt

Prologue

The letters without a name

The letters were in the catalogue. Their author was missing.

Pages of cipher lay among manuscripts in the Bibliothèque nationale de France, near material concerning Italian affairs in the early sixteenth century. A catalogue could bring a researcher to those pages. It could not tell the researcher whose words were hidden there.

George Lasry, Norbert Biermann and Satoshi Tomokiyo were looking for historical ciphers they might decipher. The unfamiliar signs were enough to interest them. They began without a famous name to guide the search.

Before you begin

A few questions.

Do I need to know cryptography?

No mathematical background is required. This is narrative history about people and hidden messages. The museum offers an optional way to explore the ideas further.

When will the book be available?

The book is forthcoming. A publication date, available editions, pricing, and purchase links will be added here when they are confirmed.

Do I need to buy the book to visit the museum?

No. Cipher Museum, its public book preview, and The Curator’s Sealed Letter are free to explore.

Yes. The museum’s book companion has reading-group questions and a suggested activity using the free reader mystery.
